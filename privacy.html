01Who we are

ScamSense is a free scam-protection app built by Karim Haidari, a cybersecurity student at the University of Tulsa. It helps people, especially older adults, spot scams in email, text messages, phone calls, websites, and payment requests. ScamSense is a prototype in testing.

In this policy, "ScamSense," "we," and "us" mean the app and its maker. "You" means the person using it.

02What stays on your device

The app saves these things only in your browser on your device:

  • Your settings: language, look (light or dark), and options you choose.
  • Your latest email scan results and your check history (what was checked and the result).
  • Your "Pause before you pay" and "I was scammed" answers, and any report documents you create.
  • Trusted family members you add (names and phone numbers or emails you type in).
  • Senders you blocked and senders you marked as real.
  • Your app PIN, if you set one. It is stored scrambled (hashed), not as plain numbers.
  • Your Outlook sign-in (a Microsoft "refresh token"), if you connect Outlook.

We cannot see any of this, except the account details in section 5. If you clear your browser data or tap Settings → Delete all ScamSense data on this device, it is gone.

03Your email

Connecting email is optional. You sign in on Google's or Microsoft's own page. ScamSense never sees your password.

What ScamSense does with your email

  • Looks at the sender, subject, text, links, attachment names, and the email's security checks (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) to decide if it is safe, suspicious, or a scam.
  • Shows you the result and what to do.
  • Sends risky emails to the AI for a second opinion (see section 4).
  • Moves or blocks an email only when you tap the button. ScamSense never sends, deletes, or edits emails, and never reads your sent mail or drafts.

Your email is read directly from Google or Microsoft into the app on your device. It is not copied to any ScamSense server. After a scan, the app tells the ScamSense server only how many emails it checked and how many were scams, for your daily report.

04AI checks and Mahi

To give a clear, plain-language answer, ScamSense uses AI from Anthropic (the maker of Claude).

What is sent

  • When you check something, or when a scan finds a risky email: the message text, sender, subject, link addresses, attachment names, the email's security check results, and the warning signs ScamSense found.
  • When you talk to Mahi: your recent chat messages, and anything Mahi looks up for you (for example, an email you asked about). Mahi may search the web to answer you.
  • When you use "Pause before you pay": your answers about the payment.

How it is sent

Normally, the request goes through the Mahi server, a small Google Apps Script program run by ScamSense. The server adds our AI key, passes the request to Anthropic, and sends the answer back. The server does not save your messages. It keeps only a count of how many answers it gave today, so it can stop at a daily limit.

If you add your own AI key under Settings → Advanced, the app sends requests straight to Anthropic with your key instead. Your key is stored only on your device, and only if you choose to remember it.

What Anthropic does with it

Anthropic processes these requests under its Commercial Terms, which say it may not train its AI models on the content sent through its API. See Anthropic's Privacy Policy for how long they keep it.

You can turn AI checks off at any time: Settings → Advanced → What the AI checks → Off. ScamSense will then use only its built-in rules, which run entirely on your device.

05Your account, reports, and alerts

To send you a daily report, warn your family when you may be in danger, and let Mahi email you, ScamSense keeps a small account for you on the ScamSense server. It holds:

  • Your first name, your email address, your language, and your report and alert choices.
  • The trusted people you add in Family: their names, emails, and phone numbers.
  • Short activity notes: the kind of thing, the result, and the time. For example "text message, scam, 3:22 PM", "scanned email: 40 emails, 2 scams", or "paused a payment". Never the message, email, or call itself.
  • A record of the danger alerts that were sent.

Your email address is confirmed once, using the Google or Microsoft sign-in you already gave the app, so nobody can use someone else's address. That sign-in is not saved on the server.

How it is used

  • Daily report: an email each morning with what ScamSense caught for you. Turn it off in Settings → Your account.
  • Danger alerts: if you say you were scammed, already paid, or were about to pay a scammer, ScamSense emails the ScamSense team and your trusted people right away, so someone can call you. You can turn off alerts to your trusted people in Settings → Your account.
  • Email me: when you ask Mahi to email you something, it is sent only to your own confirmed email.
  • Helping you: the ScamSense team can see the account list and activity notes, to know who uses ScamSense and to reach out if you may be in danger.

The account list is kept in a private Google Sheet in the ScamSense owner's Google account, and emails are sent from that account. To have your account deleted, email us (section 12).

The owner's own email report

ScamSense's maker also runs a separate script inside his own Google account that reads his own mail for a daily report. If you set up your own copy of that script, it runs only in your Google account.

06Other services the app uses

  • GitHub Pages hosts the app. Like any website host, it may log basic connection information such as your IP address. See GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Google Fonts provides the app's easy-to-read fonts.
  • Google Identity Services shows the "Sign in with Google" window when you connect Gmail.
  • Microsoft identity platform shows the Microsoft sign-in page when you connect Outlook.
  • Google Apps Script and Google Sheets run the ScamSense server (Mahi, your account, reports, and alerts) and keep the account list.

Phone numbers, websites, and crypto wallet addresses you check are judged by ScamSense's built-in rules on your device, plus the AI when it is on. ScamSense does not use analytics, trackers, or advertising tools.

07Google user data

ScamSense's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Specifically:

  • We use Gmail data only to show you scam warnings and to act on emails when you ask.
  • We do not transfer Gmail data to others, except to Anthropic's AI to check for scams (section 4), as needed to provide this feature, or if required by law. Only counts (how many emails and scams) go to the ScamSense server.
  • We do not use Gmail data for advertising.
  • No person reads your Gmail data, unless you ask us for help with a specific email, or it is needed for security or to follow the law.

08What we never do

  • Sell or rent your information.
  • Show ads or share your information with advertisers or data brokers.
  • Use your email or messages to train AI.
  • Send, delete, or change emails without you tapping a button.
  • Keep a copy of your email on a ScamSense server.

09Your choices

  • Disconnect email: Settings → Email → Disconnect.
  • Remove access completely: Google: myaccount.google.com/permissions. Microsoft: account.live.com/consent/Manage.
  • Unblock a sender: Settings → Email → Blocked senders → Unblock.
  • Turn off the daily report or alerts to your trusted people: Settings → Your account.
  • Delete your account on the server: email us at the address in section 12.
  • Turn off AI: Settings → Advanced → What the AI checks → Off.
  • Erase everything on this device: Settings → Delete all ScamSense data on this device.
  • Stop the daily report: in your ScamSense script, run stopDailyReport and stopBlocking, or delete the script.

10Security

ScamSense uses secure connections (HTTPS) everywhere, signs in only through Google's and Microsoft's own pages, asks for the least permission it needs, keeps AI keys off your device by default, and uses a strict security policy that blocks outside code from running in the app. No system is perfect. If you think something is wrong, please contact us.

11Children

ScamSense is made for adults. It is not meant for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them.
